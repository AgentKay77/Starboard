How it works

Adjusted Performance Rating

APR rewards attendance and field-relative speed, not pairwise wins. Here's how a single day's submissions become a rating change.

{# ───── Status diagram ───── #}
Step 0 · The three statuses
Completed

Time becomes a z-score against the day's field. Catastrophically slow times are clamped at the DNF floor so there's no "should I just DNF?" cliff.

DNF

Started, didn't finish. Forced to {{ '%.1f'|format(dnf_floor) }}σ.

Absent

Active player who never submitted. The floor escalates with consecutive misses: −0.5σ on the first, −1.0σ on the second, −1.5σ on the third, −2.25σ from the fourth onward. A single bad-day-off isn't catastrophic; chronic ghosting is.

{# ───── Why H2H ≠ APR ───── #}

Why head-to-head can disagree

Head-to-head only counts shared days. APR counts every day the player was active. Three things can put a less-frequent winner above a more-frequent one:

  1. Attendance. The absent floor escalates with consecutive misses (−0.5σ on the first, ramping to −2.25σ by the fourth). For a high-rated player one missed day costs ~−11 APR, three consecutive ~−66, five consecutive ~−165. Pauses sidestep this entirely.
  2. DNFs and clamped slow times. A DNF or any time below the floor costs ~−45 APR for a top player. Two of those wipe a season's worth of close wins.
  3. Field strength on shared days. Beating someone in a tight field is worth a smaller z-gap than beating them in a spread-out field. APR cares about the gap; H2H counts the win either way.
{# ───── Edge case ───── #}

Edge case · < 2 completions

A day with fewer than two completed times has no statistical signal — std_t is undefined and z-scores would be noise. Those days are skipped entirely. Nobody's rating changes, including DNFs and absentees. Penalising twelve people because one person submitted alone would be unfair.
